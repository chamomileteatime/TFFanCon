Pharma had been in Vaporex for less than — for a very short time, actually, though the exact time was a little difficult to pin down. Having just been forged and molted, he couldn't really say he'd ever spent a long time in one place. 

His stay in Altihex was fortunately very brief, and he'd only been across Iacon and Nova Cronum for what he'd hoped would be work. Vaporex was the next place on his mentor's personal agenda, all the way out in the middle of the Rust Sea.

All of that to say: though Pharma hadn't been in Vaporex for very long, he was quickly learning to miss the mainland.

It was hard to not ruminate on what returning to the mainland would look like; the research meant to be performed here could very well decide whether Pharma actually retained his temporary exemption, and he wasn't even in charge of the assignment. Helminth was forged scientific caste, light years above Pharma's rank — he cringed at the thought of leaving empty-handed.

It made him antsy. Irritable. Who would even revoke it? Helminth had been the Prime's personal physician, but even they hadn't confirmed whether the rumors circulating Nova Prime's death were true. Did they not trust him? It's not like Pharma had any plans to stir up issues regarding Nova's succession — he had no reason to call attention to himself like that. Did they just not think he was worth telling?

He hated being away from the lab because he'd always inevitably start thinking about when they'd finally decide his place here was expendable. Nevermind that he'd conducted a lot of Helminth's research, performed precision-based measurements, formatted their data... but on a temporary exemption, flung into work after a few cycles of being alive just to avoid the alternative, everything seemed liable to change.

Pharma steeled himself — he'd go back to the lab after recharge, it's not that big of a deal — and would have been perfectly content to rest, if it weren't for the company he'd discovered upon entering his apartment.

"Oh," he forced out, right before his vocalizer had to reset. "Ew."

Despite his verbalized displeasure, he didn't make any sudden movement; his gaze stayed leveled firmly at the oversized forged grub sat between the entrance and his habitation suite. As if he needed something else for Helminth to point out had gone wrong, he'd let a pest inside.

...Could grubs carry the virus? He stayed very still. Well, he had no way of knowing if that was the grub's natural plate color. Maybe if he moved a little to the side, it'd just exit and leave him well alone.

<i>The grub looked up from the valiant effort it had been making at chewing through a solid chunk of the struts of Pharma’s berth, distracted by a sound that pinged in his processor as something called “language,” not that it felt the need to clarify what exactly that meant. About as helpful as his processor tended to be—giving information with no context on how to apply it.</i>

<i>There, standing in the large, flat hole to the room, was a large, winged creature. It was similar to many of the others the grub had seen around—tall, two-legged, and brightly colored. The creatures pinged as dangerous to his systems in a way that fellow grubs never had—the grub had a gut feeling he could thank his continued existence for the fact that he had never gotten in the sights of one of those massive creatures. Until now.</i>

<i>With a startled squeak the grub leapt into action, attempting to squeeze its way into its work-in-progress hiding spot, its distended and rotund body catching on the berth above it. Eventually, the creature managed to press itself into the small space—its bright eyes visible in the darkness, watching the bot in the doorway intently.</i>

<i>The creature had not moved during its mad dash for safety—was it still a danger? Or was the creature perhaps watching him for some other purpose? Shaking, the grub pressed itself as far back into the space as it could, waiting for the creature to make the next move.</i>

He didn't react in time — as soon as Pharma realized the grub was retreating *further* into his habitation suite, it was already too late.

...Assumedly. Actually, Pharma had anticipated the grub being a little faster than it actually was. He huffed out the hot air he'd been holding in like a snort.

The grub was struggling to fit in through his doorway.

...Which surely meant it was about to molt.

He suddenly realized the... well, reality of that, and his wings snapped flat against his sides. If the grub were capable of carrying the virus... would it be better if it molted right now? Or would it molting soon prove a complication? How would he sanitize his room? How would he —

It was trying to escape the wrong way. Or hide. Hide? Could grubs even feel fear? Pharma couldn't remember anything about his grubhood, but most people couldn't.

He gave up trying to figure it out from the doorway and pressed on, slowly and methodically moving into the room with the grub, and trying to keep it within eyesight.

"Don't—ugh, you can't understand me, can you? Don't move. Don't... try to *wriggle* anywhere, alright? Just stay where you are. I'll... I'll call someone."

Would he? Would Helminth see? Would someone— maybe that was a bad idea. Maybe that was a really, really bad idea.

"Or, er..." he immediately corrected, "I'll... I'll figure you out. Why don't you just leave right out the front entrance? No need to stay at all."

Maybe he should lure it out. He moved to the side again to give it room to leave, and dug into a storage compartment for a small energon treat.

<i>The grub paused its shaking as the scent of energy reached its brain module. Energon?</i>

<i>Sure enough—the large, winged creature was now holding something small and pink in its hand. That was *definitely* energon.</i>

<i>The grub’s tanks roiled at the sensation of fuel so close. Its processor warred against itself for a long moment—every piece of code we was forged with imparted him with the knowledge that these creatures were *dangerous* to him, that they should be avoided at all cost. </i>

<i>… </i>

<i>But not as much as his code imparted on him the necessity of *fuel.* </i>

<i>The grub huffed, slowly extricating its many rounded segments out from under the berth, struggling through the not-yet-finished (and therefore rather narrow) opening. The grub had never had a negative experience with a creature before—but it had had *plenty* of positive experiences with food.</i>

<i>With a soft and inquisitive noise the grub approached the large creature, pausing several times in preparation to flee if it attempted to pounce on him. Through it all the creature remained steadfast, holding the small morsel of energon in its palm.</i>

<i>The grub closed the distance to the creature. Now close enough to feel its EM field—the grub cringed as it rolled over him, as sharp and biting against his instincts as trying to take a swim in the Rust Sea. With a delicate sniff the creature devoured the fuel straight out of its palm in a single bite, ducking back in preparation of a strike that never came.</i>

<i>The two stared at one another, energon streaked across the grub’s minuscule face.</i>
