## Chapter 01 Description
A quick explanation/disclaimer for this fic as it is part of a broader WIP Fan Continuity:

All forged Cybertronians go through an additional life stage between being “born” and being a whole guy. This stage is insectoid in nature, and grows over a period of time from about the size of a hand to the size of whatever altmode they will have. They then shed their outermost layer (think like a 3D printer) into their altmode, finally become just an average guy. 

Sparklings/Sparklets aren’t all that similar to children at all—there just like, smaller, more annoying guys. Cybertronians view them as pests because:
1. Cybertronian society gives 0 fucks about family, friends, etc.
2. They are, in fact, annoying
3. they spread diseases and chew through people’s furniture and shit

Cold-constructs are just built as guys, so they never get to experience the joys of being a pest.
## Body

Pharma had been in Vaporex for less than—

For a very, very short time—actually. The exact time was a little difficult to pin down. Newly forged—and freshly molted—as he was, he couldn't really say he'd ever spent a long time in one place. 

His stay in Altihex had fortunately been very brief, and he'd brief stays in both Iacon and Nova Cronum had only been for work. Vaporex was the next place on his mentor's personal agenda, and it was perhaps the worst yet.

Vaporex was a smaller polity, in the Southern Hemisphere of Cybertron. Being a polity on the Hydrax Plateau meant that Pharma was currently working all the way out in the middle of the Rust Sea, where the mixture  of dihydrogen monoxide and salt in the air meant that avoiding rusted joints was a constant and unending battle of futility.

All of that to say: although Pharma hadn't been in Vaporex for very long, he was quickly finding his new placement absolutely *dreadful.* He missed the mainland—more than any newly-forged bot had the right to.

It was hard for Pharma to not ruminate on what returning to the mainland would look like; the research his mentor was performing here in Vaporex could very well decide whether Pharma actually retained his temporary exemption or not. A chilling prospect when he was hardly the one in charge of the assignment. 

Helminth, his mentor, was forged into the Scientific Caste—lucky mech—light years above Pharma's rank as a Courier Caste jet. 

Pharma cringed at the thought of return to Iacon empty-handed.

It made him antsy. Irritable, even. His temporary altmode exemption relied on finding a cure to a disease that had been ravaging the Hydrax Plateau for nearly a quarter millennium. Rumors were that Chromatic Fever was already beginning to spread to the mainland, quarantine protocols finally failing. 

Pharma had only secured his exemption in the first place thanks to the rising tensions caused by the plague—anyone with even the slightest bit of medic hardware was being funneled into Cybertron’s broader medical system as quickly as possible—at least until the rising pandemic was a thing of the past. 

Safe to say he was feeling uncertain on how long his exemption would last. 

His exemption was hardly the only concern with the rising tensions—his mentor was the Prime's personal physician, or at least, he was. But Helminth had not even confirmed to their assistants whether the rumors circulating Nova Prime's death were true. Did they not trust him? It's not like Pharma had any plans to stir up issues regarding Nova's succession—he had no reason to call attention to himself like that. 

Did they just not think he was worth telling?

He hated being away from the lab. Being away would inevitably lead to thoughts about when someone would finally decide his place here was expendable. Nevermind that he'd conducted a lot of Helminth's research, performed precision-based measurements, formatted their data—

On a temporary exemption, everything was liable to change.

Pharma paused, suddenly noticing he had arrived at the door to his habsuite—he would go back to the lab after some proper recharge. He reminded himself that work without a proper defrag cycle would undoubtably be sloppy—and sloppy work was worse than no work at all. 

At least, he would have—if it weren't for the company he'd discovered upon entering his apartment.

"Oh," he paused, cycling his optics at the view before him. 

"Ew."

Despite his verbalized displeasure, he didn't dare make any sudden movement; his gaze stayed leveled firmly at the oversized pest sat between the entrance and his berth. It was large and noticeably rotund, with a white underbelly and think red plating. The black chevron on the sparklet’s forehead was smooth and shiny, a clear sign the creature was well fed. 

As if he needed something else for Helminth to point out had gone wrong—he'd let some pest inside!

Pharma paused, staring at the sparklet slowly chewing its way through his berth

...Could grubs carry the virus? He stayed very still. Well, he had no way of knowing if that was the grub's natural plate color. Maybe if he moved a little to the side, it'd just exit and leave him well alone.

<i>The grub looked up from the valiant effort it had been making at chewing through a solid chunk of the struts of Pharma’s berth, distracted by a sound that pinged in his processor as something called “language,” not that it felt the need to clarify what exactly that meant. About as helpful as his processor tended to be—giving information with no context on how to apply it.</i>

<i>There, standing in the large, flat hole to the room, was a large, winged creature. It was similar to many of the others the grub had seen around—tall, two-legged, and brightly colored. The creatures pinged as dangerous to his systems in a way that fellow grubs never had—the grub had a gut feeling he could thank his continued existence for the fact that he had never gotten in the sights of one of those massive creatures. Until now.</i>

<i>With a startled squeak the grub leapt into action, attempting to squeeze its way into its work-in-progress hiding spot, its distended and rotund body catching on the berth above it. Eventually, the creature managed to press itself into the small space—its bright eyes visible in the darkness, watching the bot in the doorway intently.</i>

<i>The creature had not moved during its mad dash for safety—was it still a danger? Or was the creature perhaps watching him for some other purpose? Shaking, the grub pressed itself as far back into the space as it could, waiting for the creature to make the next move.</i>

He didn't react in time — as soon as Pharma realized the grub was retreating *further* into his habitation suite, it was already too late.

...Assumedly. Actually, Pharma had anticipated the grub being a little faster than it actually was. He huffed out the hot air he'd been holding in like a snort.

The grub was struggling to fit in through his doorway.

...Which surely meant it was about to molt.

He suddenly realized the... well, reality of that, and his wings snapped flat against his sides. If the grub were capable of carrying the virus... would it be better if it molted right now? Or would it molting soon prove a complication? How would he sanitize his room? How would he —

It was trying to escape the wrong way. Or hide. Hide? Could grubs even feel fear? Pharma couldn't remember anything about his grubhood, but most people couldn't.

He gave up trying to figure it out from the doorway and pressed on, slowly and methodically moving into the room with the grub, and trying to keep it within eyesight.

"Don't—ugh, you can't understand me, can you? Don't move. Don't... try to *wriggle* anywhere, alright? Just stay where you are. I'll... I'll call someone."

Would he? Would Helminth see? Would someone— maybe that was a bad idea. Maybe that was a really, really bad idea.

"Or, er..." he immediately corrected, "I'll... I'll figure you out. Why don't you just leave right out the front entrance? No need to stay at all."

Maybe he should lure it out. He moved to the side again to give it room to leave, and dug into a storage compartment for a small energon treat.

<i>The grub paused its shaking as the scent of energy reached its brain module. Energon?</i>

<i>Sure enough—the large, winged creature was now holding something small and pink in its hand. That was *definitely* energon.</i>

<i>The grub’s tanks roiled at the sensation of fuel so close. Its processor warred against itself for a long moment—every piece of code we was forged with imparted him with the knowledge that these creatures were *dangerous* to him, that they should be avoided at all cost. </i>

<i>… </i>

<i>But not as much as his code imparted on him the necessity of *fuel.* </i>

<i>The grub huffed, slowly extricating its many rounded segments out from under the berth, struggling through the not-yet-finished (and therefore rather narrow) opening. The grub had never had a negative experience with a creature before—but it had had *plenty* of positive experiences with food.</i>

<i>With a soft and inquisitive noise the grub approached the large creature, pausing several times in preparation to flee if it attempted to pounce on him. Through it all the creature remained steadfast, holding the small morsel of energon in its palm.</i>

<i>The grub closed the distance to the creature. Now close enough to feel its EM field—the grub cringed as it rolled over him, as sharp and biting against his instincts as trying to take a swim in the Rust Sea. With a delicate sniff the creature devoured the fuel straight out of its palm in a single bite, ducking back in preparation of a strike that never came.</i>

<i>The two stared at one another, energon streaked across the grub’s minuscule face.</i>
