Trepan was standing beside the door, expectant and tightly-wound, when Pharma nearly broke the thing down storming inside.

The Autobots’ reluctant CMO seemed on the verge of mania, all twitching plating and wide eyes. Pharma had a reputation for being remarkably composed for a jet—even his outbursts were cut short and bitten back—so this, if Trepan were to say anything positive, was amusing.

When Pharma turned back to him, Trepan faintly regretted finding anything about this amusing. He was the only other mech in the room with his very upset, very… *Pharma*, boss.

“Did they tell you yet?” Pharma demanded, his tone remarkably steady given his erratic appearance.

Trepan debated feigning ignorance, but the temptation soured just as quickly as he’d started to regret letting his guard down. Pharma was, of course, talking about the summit Prime and his posse had with a few Decepticon leaders over their continued operations on Cybertron. With the peace treaty as it was, the two sides hadn’t been very hostile to each other—at least, not on the surface.

These “continued operations” were, given their field, scientific in nature… and Trepan knew without asking that the Decepticon-grudged Pharma would loathe sharing his materials with the other side, especially after uncovering his predecessor’s scheme.

No matter what news came out of that meeting, Pharma would’ve entered the office displeased. This displeased, though? Well, as reluctant as he was to share,  it seemed like their respective factions had made the decision for them. In their efforts against the Quintessons, and the continued survival of their race, they would be… undergoing a sort of merger.

Trepan didn’t like the idea one bit, but he was never one to complain about it, certainly not in front of his superior. He had his fun where he could, but this just wasn’t the time. Pharma looked close to smashing something against the wall, and he’d rather that not be his head.

“They did,” the surgeon answered curtly. His lips were pressed into a thin, grim smile. “Skyfire as well. It won’t surprise you neither Ambulon nor I have heard anything from him.”

If Pharma heard Trepan’s answer, he didn’t acknowledge it. Instead, he let out a loud scoff, kicked his desk with a *thud*, paused (in shame?), then paced around to stand by one of the tall cabinets.

“They know as much about what—about the… the *situation* as I do! He… *ugh*, he *gave* them… and, what, I’m just supposed to…” Pharma drew a hand down his face.

If anything, Trepan felt a little honored Pharma was being so expressive around him. It meant his boss believed them to be close. It meant Trepan had a little more freedom, that he could play it less safe.

“*Shockwave*, I have to meet—have to work with,” Pharma muttered. Then, more quietly: “I am so glad Ambulon’s out.”

Trepan let out an amused huff. “How long will it be until they slip up? Prime has to admit, we’re talking about Shockwave. Brainstorm. They’re not difficult to rile up. The lot of them wouldn’t have to do much for us to have… you know, some excuse.”

“No, I am not… I’m not going to blame them for anything. That’s ridiculous. We…” Pharma couldn’t seem to say it aloud, but Trepan could read his next words with fair accuracy: *We need their help if we want to make fast enough progress*. 

The war against the Quintessons had them all under a great amount of stress, and Pharma’s promotion had been extremely premature. Trepan felt overly-aware that Pharma was not very equipped for his position—but he’d take that to his grave if he had to.

Working with the Decepticons meant different patients, different subjects, different experiments. Different opportunities for him to develop himself… and he’d been itching to stick his needles into something for a while anyway. Autopsies and hush-hush once-in-a-blue-moon mnemosurgery trials just weren’t doing it for him anymore.

He was grateful Pharma was so enraged that he couldn’t immediately realize why Trepan wasn’t in a similar mood. Pharma only knew scraps about Trepan’s interests, but he certainly knew enough that, with a sober mind, he could scold him for malpractice.
