Rodion was, historically, an absolute mess. Quite literally at first—the polity being founded on the junction of the Old Cybertron Wasteworks, where all the junk and slag from the elevated polities to the south and southeast gathered one last time before being dumped once and for all into the Mithril Sea. It had once been a city of nothing scavengers and gutter-mechs—before a proper plate for civilized folk was built right on top of the old Wasteworks, sealing the riff-raff in with it.

Dead End was massive—the largest slum on all of Cybertron by a margin larger than the entire polity of Vos. It was not so much a place mechs ended up as one they were born into—the upper plate of Rodion, which any local would tell you might as well have been a different polity altogether—sealing in the lower plate so tightly that the slag itself gave way to a series of hotspots, starting from the heart of the polity and sloping its way eastward to the sea.

Drift winced—it was also a lawless place. As far as the civilized folk of Rodion were concerned, it wasn’t theirs to police, just a pit full of gutter-mechs and pest unwilling to hurry up and die. They must have been rather angry about the whole “not dying thing,” considering they had, once again, sent workers down to board up all exits save for wasteways.

His left arm hurt like the Pit—and for good reason. He had gotten slagged while digging for spent Syk-chips earlier in the orn. The glitches that had jumped him made off not just with all his scrap for the day, but had even succeeded in ripping his Primus-damned tire right out of his shoulder kibble. 

Drift was perhaps one of the oldest things in The Dead End. He could remember what scavenging had been like before the plate came down, at least. Well—he was mostly certain he could, memories blurred from centuries of boosters. And interfacing for comfort while on boosters. And interfacing to be able to afford said boosters. Honestly the memory issues were probably from merging while *on* boosters? He could vaguely remember hearing that code-interfacing under the influence could ruin a whole lot of fine-tuned coding. Whether that little bit of wisdom was his or someone else’s was anyone’s guess. 

Anyways—he definitely at least had *someone’s* memory on what scavenging was like before Rodion—and people didn’t *run off with other people’s shoulders* back then, that’s for damned sure.

He grumbled, struggling to force the large stretch of sheet metal blocking his own personal hole-in-the-ground while missing half of his shoulder kibble. Drift hoped his tire had better buy those glitches a quartex’s worth of fuel, just so that he could smile at the mental image of the three of them killing each other over it. 

A mech could dream.

The sheet finally gave way with an awful screech, opening just enough allowance for Drift to squeeze through the hole without welcoming anyone else in after him. He hoped he still had some proper bandages somewhere.

 The air in The Dead End wasn’t a wet and rusting, like the Hydrax Peninsula sea, nor was it hot and shimmering like the molten heat of Kaon or Tarn. But it was still just as dangerous for an open wound—static with trace radiation and metal shavings. Leaving a wound open was a guarantee for something to get caught in it as well—there were no clean surfaces in Dead End, and getting half a spent Syk-chip stuck inside a fender was a real concern around here.
