### For Rain <3

Stanix was grey—grey buildings, grey sky, grey liquid lapping at the shores. It had been built entirely around its ports. Most of the polity worked in shipbuilding, or only lived in the cities in the short times they weren’t either at sea or up amongst the stars. It was a grey polity, and also a very empty one.

It was also an *old* polity–great skyscrapers which sat mostly empty, streets built for a population that only a fraction of which was in the city at any given time. Oilhouses and other forms of entertainment became sparce as one moved away from the docks and space-lanes. In short, it was cold, and dark, and dreary.

It served Ravage just fine.

A shrill cry sounded out as Laserbeak circled overhead. The youngling had only just shed its molting, and with it had traded their energetic squirming for the freedom of the skies. 

They were heading to a fuel cache Ravage knew was in the area. Fuel was always tight for the three of them, and there were very little options for sparklets and Undesirable-Caste Cybertronians. All three of them were hungry. Laserbeak’s shed meant that Ravage could finally travel long distances again. This fuel cache would be her first stop.

The cache belonged to The Femmes, and whatever other rebellious groups they were working with nowadays. It was hard to keep track. Ravage hardly associated with them anymore, but she had been a member, back in her youth. She still followed their general belief system and code of conduct. Her leaving had been over personal reasons. 

She tended to consider herself more of a lone operative, anyways.

The cache was meant for members down on their luck. Femmes tended to struggle to find work, even as far from Iacon as Stanix. It was the downside of wearing their rebellious aspirations on their frame. They took it with pride, helping one another with resources when they could. There was a very real chance they would arrive to find it empty, but Ravage doubted it. There was always someone in need of aid. 

Ravage liked to imagine that even if they knew she was skimming off the top, they wouldn’t mind. Hopefully. That “Social Bond”, or whatever they were calling it nowadays. Helping those in need was part of their code. Ravage was in plenty of need, even if their falling out was on… less than pleasant circumstances.

Ravage huffed as Laserbeak took another playful swoop towards her, poising their claws to attack only to abort the motion and streak past at the last second, cackling in glee. Laserbeak had always been the more troublesome of her charges.

The other—not yet molted and therefore still unnamed—sat heavy in her jaws, squirming in discomfort as its sibling swooped at it again and again. 

The sparklet was small, small enough for Ravage to still hold some small amount of hope that it might grow into some sort of beast-class Cybertronian—something approachable like a cybercat or a turbofox. 

She was deluding herself. Despite the creature’s small size it was *heavy.* Heavy in a way Ravage knew meant *mass displacement.* 

Her littlest sparklet was going to be a mech, whether she liked it or not.

Ravage sighed internally. She had intended to be choosy in her choice of sparklets. Laserbeak had been obvious—a prominent beak and stubby little sparklet legs that gave way to wing-nubs as they grew. They had been cute, in a hideous sort of way. One look and Ravage had known that little sparklet was going to grow into a beast-class Cybertronian, just like herself.

Her littlest had seemed obvious, too. It shifted it’s helm to turn to her, and Ravage could see the delicate audial processes that had tricked her—pointed and broad just like her own. She knew she was being unreasonable. Pointed audial processes were hardly a discerning feature when it came to root-modes. But then she had been so *hopeful,* so desperate, for someone like her. 

She was one-of-a-kind. A theoretical concept. A cold-constructed prototype that never saw mass-production. 

It had blinded her better judgment.

She stepped delicately across the thin gap between two buildings. Fuel had been extra scarce for the three of them, recently. Laserbeak had just finished molting. Her youngest was just about to start it. And molting meant more fuel, and less traveling.

Ravage sighed around the heavy weight in her mouth. Sparklets did just fine on their own. There was a reason taking them in—as Ravage had—was outside the norm. They were wild animals, and would be until they finished molting.

Or, at least, they were supposed to be. Laserbeak had been. Her littlest seemed a little too pleased with companionship for a sparklet, happy to let Ravage take care of it and carry it around wherever. 

That quiet little voice of anger inside her roiled at sparklet held in her jaws. Why had she bothered? She had worked hard to see this specific sparklet to its molting. 

Sparklets were no commodity, and they were treated as such. If she hadn’t bothered with the sparklet there was still a reasonable chance it would’ve survived to its molting. Not a great chance, but it could. Every other sparklet in the world was going through it.

No, it was special treatment—treatment she had given with purpose to someone who she *thought* would turn out like her. 

Not a mech. 

What would they be like, she wondered. Would they kick her to the curb? Run off to be some politician or noble-mech, and leave her behind? 

A waste—that’s what that quiet voice told her this was. She had wasted her resources and time getting a faulty sparklet across the finish line. 

All that work, for a mech! Special treatment, for a Cybertronian who would only go on receive it for the rest of their life. 

Her sparklet squirmed uncomfortably in her mouth, as if reacting to her darkened thoughts. It was right, of course. Such thoughts were unbecoming of her. 

Laserbeak swooped in for another pass, and Ravage growled at them as they went by.

*::That’s enough, Laserbeak::* she sent over her short-range comms. They were still learning how to speak properly, but they could understand her just fine.

Laserbeak trilled in frustration, turning overhead in tight circles. They wanted to play. The sparklet in her mouth eyed them warily. Its developing plating fluttered. 

A small flap, no larger than Ravage’s optic, fluttered open and then closed. 

That was the other thing. The space under the flap had been slowly hollowing itself out overtime. The firm plating was giving way to the plush grey internals underneath. The sparklet had even begun to leak line energon through the hollow space. It was disgusting to clean, and Ravage regretting her choices every time it happened. The sparklet would cry and squeal through the entire process, souring Ravage’s mood. It wasn’t even supposed to hurt, was the confusing part. The cause of the sparklet’s upset was indiscernible. 

Unlike a pointy audial, a leaky hollow *was* considered discernable kibble. It would develop into a dock any day now.

And a dock meant a carrier. Data Caste. *Disposable.* 

Her little mechlet would only be a half-step up the social chain. It was a ridiculous thought to look down on her sparklet for that. They would be serving the same masters, soon enough. 

Both her little sparklets would be born forged, at least. She was lucky that her own creation status did not matter as much as it should. Ravage was a prototype. There was no one like her. And in a society built on anonymity and individualism, being one-of-a-kind was almost as good as being forged. They could not comprehend why someone would bother to cold-construct an *animal*—let alone someone with a animal root-mode. They rarely ever stopped to check. 

Laserbeak’s EMF flared out in panic. Ravage paused. Pulsing their EMF outwards was the only form of communication Laserbeak had mastered. It was frustrating at times, as it didn’t exactly clarify why the youngling was experiencing specific emotions. The drop-point wasn’t far now, only a few more blocks to the east—

An audial-rattling roar. A flier shooting by. Her sparklet cried out in fear as she braced against the ground in the following gust of wind. 

The strange EMF that passed above her vibrated with cruel mirth. 

Ravage paused, terrified. Her plating pressed itself tight against her frame. Her spark spun wildly as her vents flew open, sucking in cold air in preparation of a fight.

The jet was long gone.

Ravage pulled up a local map from her memory-banks. It was a little out-of-date, here and there, but it was functional. There was an airlane not far from here, but no fliers were supposed to fly that low. She would know—she had had an on-and-off fling with a jet once upon a time, and she had explained air laws at length.

The sky was quiet.

She shot up, pinging urgent messages across her short-range comms. That flier had gone off-course for a reason. Something cruel and sinister, if she was reading their EMF correctly. Ravage scanned the skies, looking for her youngling.

After a few spark-quaking moments, Laserbeak flew in low, landing at her left. The youngling was shaking. 

Ravage looked them over, field wrapping the youngling in relief and comfort as best she could. Laserbeak’s EMF rattled against her own. She forced herself to project as much calm as she could. Her tail lashed wildly behind her.

“Stay close to me!” she barked. Laserbeak flinched as guilt flooded her lines. The youngling was no-doubt confused, potentially even blaming themself for what had just happened. 

It wasn’t their fault they had almost been hit. There were plently of jets that made a game out of hitting mechanimals, she knew. Ground vehicles as well. Most people assumed they had the decency to not extend the same curtesy to beast-class Cybertronians, but Ravage had learned better herself long ago. 

Laserbeak was uninjured, at least. She was fast and small, built for dodging and tight aerial maneuvers a jet could never accomplish.

Ravage huffed, attempting to push her emotions down. There was nothing that could be done.

She placed her sparklet on the ground, leaning forward to press her helm against the youngling’s. Laserbeak trilled in sorrow against her. 

The two sat there for a moment, the sparklet watching consideringly, until Laserbeak leaned away with a flutter on their wings.

The youngling took a couple of hops forward. They were communicated that they were ready to continue on their journey. However, Laserbeak’s former mirth was gone. They would not be confident in flying freely for a while. Ravage’s spark ached for them. It was a hard lesson to learn.

She eyed her sparklet as she when to lift it once again. There was a silver lining to all of this, something that could kept Ravage going. 

A carrier. 

Ravage was small, and a mono-former. She looked to the young flier hopping along ahead of her. So was Laserbeak. 

If her little sparklet was a carrier, then it would no longer be impossible to get a reformatting. 

Ravage had never had the shanix for a proper reformatting. It was not the sort of thing you could get on the down low from disreputable surgeons. Not for herself, let alone her and Laserbeak. 

Some of the larger and more social polities, like Tetrahex or Hydrax, were known for offering monetary assistance for reformatting for mono-formers. The issue was that said assistance relied on proof of work. Ravage had always scoffed at that. You needed proof of function in order to be given a function. Ridiculous. It was obvious who the system benefitted. Ravage was a beast-class Cybertronian, part of the Undesirable Caste. No one was giving her a job. No, reformatting assistance was reserved for pretty little mechs to get pushed out of the Undesirable Caste as quickly as possible, leaving all the hideous little beasties behind. 

Laserbeak hopped along beside her as she continued her trek. Every so often they would huff, flapping their wings. Then, with one considering look towards the sky, they would shake their head, and continue hopping along behind her. It slowed their pace down significantly, but she allowed the youngling their terror. Laserbeak was newly molted, they had never experienced being seen as *lesser.* Their worldview was no doubt in tatters. Ravage would be there when they were ready, but fuel was the priority right now. 

It would be different for them if a cassette approached them for a reformatting. Her little mechlet could ask for Ravage and Laserbeak to be reformatted into their first cassettes. It was a loophole built on the crossroads of two different prejudices. 

Ravage was a beast, an animal—but cassettes weren’t *people*, they were *tools*. 

A carrier rounding up a few barely-sentient mechanimals to be their cassettes? Better than bending *real* Cybertronians under their will. 

Freedom was the right of all civilized beings, and what was less civilized than an animal?

That had always been the issue with her, her creators had once said—she was too certain of her own personhood. She supposed they might have had a point. Her insistence on her own freedom had not served her well so far. Stanix was dreary and dark and quiet—but it also functioned almost entirely on social welfare. Ravage had been slumming it in the streets for so long particularly because said social welfare was not open to her kind. 

Perhaps she could’ve worked for some exemption. Cozy up to some noble or lawmaker and see if they ever considered her for spy work. A few mods later, and her exemption up to disposable class would’ve been secured. But that was not who she was.

She had been close to an exemption, once. An old romantic tryst, one that would’ve secured her a spot on the Ark and a military exemption. There were nights she wondered why she had turned Cyclonus down. It had been about Cybertron, she remembered. She was young, had wanted to make a difference. It was a decision that festered in the times when fuel was scarce. Or when she was feeling particularly nostalgic about the jet.

If–and it was still only an “if,” but Ravage had not survived this long without planning ahead—if her little sparklet was developing into a carrier, that meant things would be changing.  Ravage’s existence would no longer be her own, true, but would it be worth it?

Disposable Caste mechs did not do much better in Stanix. But any mech had a chance to be offered a job, at least. That was how the Functionists worked. And carriers were never in want of work. A single mech’s pay for three or four pair of hands? It was a steal. And with said job, they could apply for Ravage and Laserbeak to be their cassettes. 

And then Ravage would cease existing as a person, and begin her life as an accessory.

She thought it should have bothered her more. She *liked* being an individual. Thrived on it, even. 

Cyclonus had often said that if Ravage were anything other than a beast-class mono-former, the lowest of the low, she would have been climbing the ranks faster than anyone could stop her. Been the jets superior before the other could even blink. But as she was, no one had ever offered her a chance. She hadn’t held a job since the day the Senate passed that law deciding clearing cold-constructed Cybertronian to be designed with mech root-modes. She was a living reminder of historic sensibilities, not fit for a modern era.

That was what had drawn her to the Cyber-Organic Movement in the first place, even if she had left on bad terms. The message had been fantastic—pushing back against Cybertronian Imperialism. Down with Nova Prime’s superiority complex! The loss of the Ark had been personal for her. 

Then the movement changed, took on a new image. 

“The Femmes”, they called themselves now. Something about recreating organic biological norms, proving Cybertronians were only different from organics on a surface level. Heretical, by Functionist standards. 

They did not understand her frustration then, pleased with their shiny new paint schemes and slim figures. Her leaving was a petty argument in the eyes of most of her peers. How was an *animal* to replicate the look? She had asked them. She had equal access to their underground metallurgists and mod-makers as the rest of them, of course. They couldn’t understand that that wasn’t the issue. No one looked at an animal with a slim waist and went “Oh, a Femme!” No matter what changes she made—and she had made several—she was still just an animal in their eyes. 

That was a privilege reserved for *real* Cybertronians.

So yes—she assumed she would’ve been more resistant to the idea of living the rest of her existence as someone else’s accessory. Ravage had never dared consider it in the past. She hated mechs. Putting her trust in one to the degree of binding herself to them permanently, that was too much. 

But this was her sparklet. She tilted her head consideringly. She had raised him, cared for him. Provided for him in a way not unlike a carrier would be expected to care for her.

Her HUD pinged with a fuel warning.

Stanix ran on social welfare. Fuel could not be bought, it was provided, other than those frilly little specialty drinks sold in the oil houses that Ravage was not welcome inside. 

A function was a requirement for fuel, just as it was for housing or for a reformatting. Ravage had survived this long scavenging for her fuel, be that in oilhouse dumpsters or stealing it off of unsuspecting idiots. 

Or, in the particularly hard months, siphoning. 

She shivered. Ravage had never gone beyond siphoning the odd sparklet, of course. Something like that on her record would have her recalled immediately. 

The decision had made fuel more scarce, but she had stopped siphoning from sparklets too, recently. She had tried. Not long after her adoption of her two little sparklets, she had tried. It had not gone well. One look into it’s panicked eyes—the squeals it made as it bled…

Ravage never tried that again.

Her soon-to-be mechlet squirmed in her mouth. It’s faint proto-EMF flared in something that Ravage could delude herself was comfort. It would be a stifling future, no doubt. Her little carrier-let would be raised to the best of her ability, but that did not confirm that they would have the right drive to be a good carrier. And even if they were, her life would belong to a mech’s whose life belonged to someone else. Disposable Caste was only one tier up from where she was now. 

She scoffed—her friends of the past would hardly recognize her now. Cyclonus, The Femme Movement—time and time again Ravage had traded opportunities for the sake of retaining her individuality. It was all she had. Cyclonus had understood that, in the end. The Femmes hadn’t. She had been abandoned by her makers, by her species, her freedom was the only thing anyone had every granted her. Throwing it away was an affront to everything she had ever sacrificed.

And her she was, considering it for a half-step forward. Cyclonus’ offer had been a better deal, and she had refused it outright. 

But, as she looked at Laserbeak fluttering on ahead—she found herself almost happy with the idea.
