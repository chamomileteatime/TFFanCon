## Chapter 01 Description
A quick explanation/disclaimer for this fic as it is part of a broader WIP Fan Continuity:

All forged Cybertronians go through an additional life stage between being “born” and being a whole guy. This stage is insectoid in nature, and grows over a period of time from about the size of a hand to the size of whatever altmode they will have. They then shed their outermost layer (think like a 3D printer) into their altmode, finally become just an average guy. 

Sparklings/Sparklets aren’t all that similar to children at all—their brain is just as developed as any other Cybertronian, just without the life experience to apply it.

Cold-constructs are just built as guys, so they never get to experience the joys of being a pest.

## Body

Pharma had been in Vaporex for less than…

…For a very, very short time—actually. The exact time was a little difficult to pin down. Newly forged—and freshly molted—as he was, he couldn't really say he'd ever spent a long time in one place. 

His stay in Altihex had fortunately been very brief, and he'd brief stays in both Iacon and Nova Cronum had only been for work. Vaporex was the next place on his mentor's personal agenda, and it was perhaps the worst yet.

Vaporex was a smaller polity, in the Southern Hemisphere of Cybertron. Being a polity on the Hydrax Plateau meant that Pharma was currently working all the way out in the middle of the Rust Sea, where the mixture  of dihydrogen monoxide and salt in the air meant that avoiding rusted joints was a constant and unending battle of futility.

All of that to say: although Pharma hadn't been in Vaporex for very long, he was quickly finding his new placement absolutely *dreadful.* He missed the mainland—more than any newly-forged bot had the right to.

It was hard for Pharma to not ruminate on what returning to the mainland would look like; the research his mentor was performing here in Vaporex could very well decide whether Pharma actually retained his temporary exemption or not. A chilling prospect when he was hardly the one in charge of the assignment. 

Helminth, his mentor, was forged into the Scientific Caste—lucky mech—light years above Pharma's rank as a Courier Caste jet. 

Pharma cringed at the thought of return to Iacon empty-handed.

It made him antsy. Irritable, even. His temporary altmode exemption relied on finding a cure to a disease that had been ravaging the Hydrax Plateau for nearly a quarter millennium. Rumors were that Chromatic Fever was already beginning to spread to the mainland, quarantine protocols finally failing. 

Pharma had only secured his exemption in the first place thanks to the rising tensions caused by the plague—anyone with even the slightest bit of medic hardware was being funneled into Cybertron’s broader medical system as quickly as possible—at least until the rising pandemic was a thing of the past. 

Safe to say he was feeling uncertain on how long his exemption would last. 

His exemption was hardly the only concern with the rising tensions—his mentor was the Prime's personal physician, or at least, he was. But Helminth had not even confirmed to their assistants whether the rumors circulating Nova Prime's death were true. Did they not trust him? It's not like Pharma had any plans to stir up issues regarding Nova's succession—he had no reason to call attention to himself like that. 

Did they just not think he was worth telling?

He hated being away from the lab. Being away would inevitably lead to thoughts about when someone would finally decide his place here was expendable. Nevermind that he'd conducted a lot of Helminth's research, performed precision-based measurements, formatted their data—

On a temporary exemption, everything was liable to change.

Pharma paused, suddenly noticing he had arrived at the door to his habsuite—he would go back to the lab after some proper recharge. He reminded himself that work without a proper defrag cycle would undoubtably be sloppy—and sloppy work was worse than no work at all. 

At least, he would have—if it weren't for the company he'd discovered upon entering his apartment.

"Oh," he paused, cycling his optics at the view before him. 

"Ew."

He didn't dare make any sudden movement; his gaze stayed leveled firmly at the oversized pest sat between the entrance and his berth. 

It was large and noticeably rotund, with a white underbelly and think red plating. The black chevron on the sparklet’s forehead was smooth and shiny, a clear sign the creature was well fed. 

As if he needed something else for Helminth to point out had gone wrong—he'd let some *pest* inside!

Pharma paused, staring at the sparklet slowly chewing its way through his berth. Could sparklets also be carriers for Chromatic Fever? He had no way of knowing if that was the grub's natural plate color—the clearest sign of an infected mech.

The grub looked up from the valiant effort it had been making at chewing through a solid chunk of the struts of Pharma’s berth.

With a startled squeak the grub scurried away from him, attempting to squeeze its way into its work-in-progress hiding spot, its rotund body catching on the sides of the hole. Eventually, the creature managed to press itself into the small space.

The two Cybertronians stared at each other for a long moment—the sparklet’s bright eyes visible in the darkness, watching the bot in the doorway intently.

Pharma hadn’t reacted in time—as soon as he realized the sparklet was retreating *further* into his habitation suite, it was already too late.

...Assumedly. Actually, Pharma had anticipated the sparklet being a little faster than it actually was. He huffed out the hot air he'd been holding in a muted snort.

The oversized sparklet was struggling to fit in through the hole it had made in the underside of his berth.

...Which surely meant it was about to molt.

He suddenly realized the... well, reality of that, and his wings snapped flat against his sides. A sparklet had made itself a nest in his own habsuite, one about to molt at that. It was definitely large enough—about the size of a standard vehicle altmode and ugh, wasn’t that a thought. The little pest currently inhabiting his habsuite and ruining his berth could very well be his *intellectual superior* in just a matter of years.

Pharma scoffed at the sparklet. It was trying to escape the wrong way, retreating deeper into the space it had carved out under his berth. Was it trying to hide? Could grubs even feel fear? Pharma couldn't remember anything about the time before his own molting, but then again most people couldn't.

With a jolt Pharma realized that he and the sparklet in front of him could not be more than a thousand years apart in age, the jet still carrying grooved plating from his own molting in the harder-to-reach corners of his frame. It was a humanizing thought—had he been like this, only a dozen or so centuries ago?

He gave up trying to figure the pest out from the doorway and pressed on, slowly and methodically moving into the room with the sparklet, trying his best to keep it within eyesight at all times.

"Don't—ugh, you can't understand me, can you? Don't move. Don't... try to *wriggle* anywhere, alright? Just stay where you are. I'll... I'll call someone."

Would he? Would Helminth be able to see if he did? What would his mentor think of him? Maybe calling someone was a bad idea. Maybe that was a really, really bad idea.

"Or… er,” he corrected, continuing in spite of his certainty that the being in front of him could not understand him "I'll... I'll figure it out. Why don't you just leave right out the front entrance? No need to stay at all."

Maybe he could lure it out. He moved to the side again, pressing himself against the wall of the habsuite, to give it room to leave, and dug into a storage compartment for a small energon treat.

The grub paused in its shaking at the scent of energon.

The grub squeaked, slowly extricating its many rounded segments out from under the berth, struggling through the not-yet-finished—and therefore rather narrow—opening.

With a soft and inquisitive noise the grub approached him, pausing several times in preparation to flee if Pharma were to attempt to pounce at it. Through it all he remained steadfast, holding the small morsel of energon in his palm.

The grub closed the distance to the creature. Now close enough to feel its EM field, Pharma cringed as it rolled over him, as sharp and biting against his own as trying to take a swim in the Rust Sea. With a delicate sniff the creature devoured the fuel straight out of its palm in a single bite, ducking back in preparation of a strike that never came.

The two stared at one another, energon streaked across the grub’s minuscule face.
