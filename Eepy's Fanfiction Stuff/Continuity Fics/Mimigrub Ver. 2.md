Dominus Ambus and Rewind arrived at the Scorched Caves in the early joors of the morning. Rewind could only tell the time from the ping of his internal chronometer. The steep cave walls above blocked out the morning sun.

The hotspot was empty, save for them and the census drones, cataloging and scanning the sleeping grubs tucked into alcoves all around them. Rewind makes eye contact with one of the pests. The large red creature yawned and rolled over. Rewind cringed.

“Excuse me, sir,” he inquired. “Why are we here, exactly?” Dominus had hardly given him any explanation when telling the data slug to awaken this morning—simply that he needed assistance in some House matters.

“It’s a mandated sweep,” Dominus explained. “It has been decided that the House of Ambus has been too small for too long.”

Well that was intentionally rather vague.

“Can you explain?” Rewind asked, somewhat petulantly. If he was here to assist the other, the very least the noble could provide him with was some sort of explanation.

“It’s a matter pertaining to our House’s nobility exemption.” Dominus rubbed at his facial kibble. “There is a certain population size a noble house is expected to meet.”

Ah, that explained it. Rewind doubted that a single member met any sort of population expectations.

Still, this was the first Rewind had heard of such a practice. The nobility of Cybertron were a secretive bunch. This was the first mandated sweep Dominus had been sent on since his… procurement of the data slug.

Rewind shivered at the reminder—he preferred not to think of his previous owners, nor the short period of time in which he had been slated for disposal. It hurt him to admit, but Dominus’ decision to acquire a disposable servant in need of refurbishment instead of a fresh new product had been the best thing to ever happen to him. He supposed not many good things happened in a Disposable’s life.

The two travelled the caves in silence for a time, Dominus guiding the data slug toward the central room.

The cave system housing the hotspot was bleak. The tall, smooth walls of the cave were cast a deep red by the pits of molten rock that bubbled in the far corners of the large open space at the core of the cave system. The Scorched Caves was one of the deepest hotspots on Cybertron. Part of why it had remained so active for so long. A large computer spread out across the central room, its fire-proof wiring stretching out like a mural across the ground. Census drones came and went, buzzing through the air. Sparklets scrabbled out from under dense wiring as they passed, ducking from underfoot.

Dominus came to a stop at the central computer. With a press of his servo the computer turns on. Rewind was deeply curious about the census databanks kept here. All but the nobility and the government were barred from accessing them.

Dominus waved Rewind over.

“Are there any specific specifications you are looking for?” Rewind asked, looking around the large space. Dominus handed him a data slug.

“This is the search query to run. It is encrypted.”

Rewind tilted his helm at the other. “Do you not trust me to run it?” He asked, curious. “You can share the specifics with me, remember? I’m Disposable-Caste, your property—I would be disposed of for good if I did anything against my owner’s wishes.”

Dominus looked visibly uncomfortable at being reminded of their relationship. Rewind rolled his optics internally. It was infuriating how uncomfortable Dominus was with the fact of owning another Cybertronian. Uncomfortable enough to avoid the topic, but not uncomfortable to set Rewind free.

Dominus, however, continued to remain tight-lipped about the query. He stood silently, brow furrowed. Anxious for Rewind to provide him with results, no doubt.

Rewind shrugged, plugging the slug into his arm. The encrypted executable worked quickly, beginning a search of the entire system’s databanks. Rewind tilted his helm when he noticed how oddly the query interacted with the census databanks. It went through, obviously, but it was set to deleting records just as quickly as it was making them. Immediately it was replacing the queries with a much more basic search. Something about specific color patterns. Rewind could not see any deeper into the query without a full decryption.

Rewind noted that the code itself–although he could only see what it is doing secondhand—must have been archaic, at least twice the age of Dominus himself.

The databank pings—a match to the query had been found.

Rewind turned to relay this information to Dominus, “There’s a match, sir.”

Dominus flinched, whipping around from where he had been studying the rock face. He opened his mouth, but nothing emerged.

With a hiss of static he reset his vocalizer. “Double check.”

Rewind paused, confused.

Why would Dominus be so shocked to find a match? Was that not what they were here for? Most noble houses were several sizes of magnitude larger than the House of Ambus. Rewind had assumed Dominus simply wasn’t interested in new members. Was he missing something deeper?

Rewind rechecked the results, and under his deeper scrutiny he noticed with a jolt that the sparklet in question is marked as “to-cull.” He pauses. He dug a little deeper—deeper than a datastick like him could possibly be allowed to. The tags the machine provided to him were three emotionless words:

Size, Superfluous Altmode.

Strange.

Dominus cleared his throat.

Rewind quickly returned to the tab he had left. “It definitely says here that there’s a match, sir,” he began, “but it looks like the census drones lost the sparklet somewhere where the cave system narrows out north of here.” But that was a vorn ago—there aren’t any more recent details.

Dominus raised a servo to his chin consideringly. “Thank you for your assistance, my friend.” Rewind attempted to disguise how he bristled at the meaningless pleasantry. Friend. ”Your expertise will no longer be needed. If you could send the coordinates to my commlink and return to the estate, that would be very much appreciated.”

Rewind hesitated, debating if it is in his place to argue with the bot in front of him who—regardless of his meaningless politeness—is still his owner. At least for the time being. He steeled himself, preparing to push his luck.

“I don’t know if that’s wise–” he cringed, rewording his thoughts to be less abrasive–”it says here that the drones lost the sparklet in this specific corridor,” he argued, sending Dominus the specifications of the cave—including its extremely narrow width. Too narrow for Dominus. “It looks like you wouldn’t be able to fit through this portion here. I can.”

Dominus shook his head. “I can handle it,” he insisted.

Rewind knew better than to push any harder. Let the other be vague—he could figure out how to squeeze through a gap a quarter his width by himself.

Rewind left The Scorched Caves confused. The walk to the nearest transportation hub was long and lonely. He boarded the rail in the dead of night—the car almost empty, save for him and the last stragglers making their way home. The long walk back to the Disposable Car was filled with accusatory looks as he pushed past. Without Dominus though, it’s where he was expected to go. The Disposable Caste car was standing room only—especially unfortunate considering that most Data-Class Cybertronians are on the small side, making every harsh stop of the train into an ordeal in escaping with a minimal number of dents.

When the door opened there were two other data slugs. One of them nodded to him. He nodded back.

He pushed past to wedge himself against one of the walls, looping his arm in one of the low bars meant to prevent falls. It won’t stop him from crashing into the ground on a sharp turn, but it will keep him upright against the normal bumps of the train.

He pulls up the digital imprint he had made of the census records. He hopes that he can find out what specific “superfluous altmode” Dominus had been searching for. He compares the list of the hotspot’s most common altmodes. It takes his symptom a joor to compile a readable graph. He’s in desperate need of a good recharge cycle. After some time watching cities pass from the window of the train car, his processor pings him with the completed chart. He checks it:

Standard-Class Vehicles, Convoys, and Construction-Class Vehicles.

None of the numbers match up to the cull records Rewind had found—the culled altmodes were clearly not being reported for census records. Another oddity.

Rewind had been fascinated by the records at first. They were perhaps the most well documented records the data slug had ever seen. It made sense, in retrospect, if these were the records the functionalists used to provide proper ratios of altmodes. But, looking deeper into the code, Rewind was horrified by the sheer number of “deformed” grubs that were being culled on the regular. He couldn’t see the exact altmodes—or any other reasonings for the cullings—but he could see where the numbers didn’t add up. The amount of sentio metallico being made was one and a half times the number of registered Cybertronians from this hotspot in the last vorn, at least. Rewind shivered. That was a whole additional third of the population—gone. Were the functionalists doing this to all their hotspots? Was it a matter of function? Or—and Rewind truly shook at this—had the Functionalists been lying about the amount of resources Cybertron had left?

The train comms announced his stop.

There was no formal stop in Ambustus Minor. Instead, Rewind had to get off at a stop a several-joor walk away, and make the trek up to the manor himself.

The trek back to the Ambus Estate happened in complete silence. Rewind double and triple checked the numbers, but nothing added up in a way that told him anything he hadn’t already figured out. What could Dominus—what could Cybertron—be hiding?

He entered the estate, engine revving at both the cold air and emptiness inside. The ornate doors slammed shut behind him. A thin layer of dust was visible on the ground from where Rewind stood, several heads lower than the average mech. The silence had bothered him greatly when he had first been taken to the estate. Lifeless, empty, and cold were the first words to come to mind.

Rewind started towards his quarters, but hesitated. He replayed Dominus’ instructions to him, but they provide no clarity. Would Dominus prefer him to wait in his quarters? Or in the visiting room? He decided that in lieu of an answer it would be best to assume the visiting room—here at the front of the manor. Better for Dominus to be expecting him in his quarters and find him upon entering, than expecting Rewind at the entrance only to find him missing.

He went to sit, before thinking better of it. Leaning against the wall, he set up a ping to warn him the moment someone attempted to enter the manor, and booted up the script for a short data-bank defrag cycle.

If there was anything Rewind was thankful for in being forged a data slug it was that—unique coding allowing him a half-recharge half-processing state where he could sort through memories and acquired data for sorting and tagging. It meant that the data-slug had to take more off-hours than the average mech. But for Rewind it was his only solace, a single sphere in which he ruled supreme, no owners or masters to correct his tagging structure or demand that he reformat thousands of years of records from chronological to alphabetical, ugh—

Rewind let himself be absorbed into the process of sorting and tagging.

---

Rewind came-to with a jolt. Pinging his chronometer, the data slug found that several days had passed—significantly longer than he was expecting Dominus to take. Sure enough, Dominus entered in through the ornate door a moment later.

He jumped, catching Rewind out of the corner of his optics.

Dominus cleared his vocalizer with a hiss of static.

“How long have you been waiting here?” He asked, optical ridge furrowed in concern.

Rewind shrugged. He doubted the noble truly cared—for many of them any act of care towards their property was nothing more than a show of humility to their peers.

He had come to trust—to some degree, at least—Dominus wasn’t like that. But the past orn had done much to shake his trust in the other. He found himself more skeptical of his owner than he had been before.

It is then that Rewind noticed the bundle in Dominus’ servos.

“Is that–” he asked, motioning toward the wrappings of mesh.

“Yes,” Dominus cut him off forcefully, pulling the bundle closer to his spark.

Rewind blinked. The sparklet itself was not visible amongst the blankets of mesh. It seemed to be an intentional choice, from the way Dominus held the bundle as if Rewind might rip it from his arms.

“I secured the Sparklet.” Dominus continued. “You may call for the Metallurgist. Tell them they are expected on the premises immediately.”

“May I see it?” The words were out of his vocalizer before he even thought of them. Dominus flinched.

With a deep ex-vent, Dominus forced his tightened struts to relax. He looked at Rewind sadly.

“No. You may not.” Dominus confirmed apologetically. “Not until the proper procedures have been seen to.”

Rewind tilted his helm, confused. Dominus continued.

“It’s tradition—I apologize,” he stated, “No one is permitted to see the sparklet until it has been made a proper member of the House of Ambus.”

With that Dominus pushed past him, the bundle still tight in his arms. The minesweeper walked briskly towards the lift, disappearing from Rewind’s view as quickly as his pedes would take him.

Rewind sent a ping to the Metallurgist.

The whole situation was strange, and Rewind had to force himself to shrug it off as noble weirdness.

A ping. His systems have finished decrypting the encrypted search query.

It is time to do some digging of his own.
